Brownstone

A visual direction for Flats where light does the storytelling: a cold moonlit room that grows warmer, lamp by lamp, as you keep showing up.

1Summary

Brownstone keeps everything Flats already does and changes how it looks and feels. Every friend has a flat in one brownstone on a wet night street. From outside, each window glows brighter with that week's workouts. Inside, the flat is drawn as a detailed, warmly lit room in the existing one-point cutaway.

The central idea is that light is the progress bar. A new flat is lit almost entirely by cold moonlight through an uncurtained window. Each light you unpack cuts a warm pool out of the dark. Paint, curtains and flooring change the whole room at once. By the time the flat is homey, a dozen small warm sources do the work and the moonlight has become a quiet accent you have shaped with your own curtains.

Three rules hold it together. The window light never goes away. Every lamp is a real light. Nothing is ever taken from you.

2What the game does today

Brownstone is a skin and lighting system over the current rules. None of these change:

  • Logging. One tap, "I worked out today", once per day.
  • Presents. Each workout earns a present. Its tier comes from that week's count: the first workout gives tier 1, the second and third tier 2, the fourth and later tier 3. Each present offers three choices.
  • Items. 50 catalogue items, each with zones (ceiling, wall, wallTop, window, sill, floor, rug), a rarity (standard, seasonal, surprise) and palettes with primary, shade and accent colour roles. Twelve surfaces cover wallpaper and flooring.
  • Pet. A cat, dog, bunny, frog or duck. Its mood is perky after a workout today, content otherwise, and sleepy after seven quiet days.
  • Plants. Grow through stages 0, 1 and 2, at 2 and 5 workouts after unpacking.
  • Cosiness title. Earned by items placed: Bare little box (0), Moving in (1), Getting homey (3), Snug nook (6), Cosy nest (9), Dreamy den (13), Storybook flat (17).
  • Visits. Friends leave a heart, star or treat, one of each per day. Stars stay on the window frame. The pet eats treats the next time the owner opens the app.
  • No punishment. Missed weeks never remove anything.

3Visual language

Detailed, adult and warm, closer to a lamplit period drama than a cartoon. It is always night, so lamps matter. Rooms read as dark academia: panelled walls, leather, brass, books, velvet, plants.

Materials

Cognac leather, brass, walnut, oxblood and mustard velvet, cream knit, terracotta, rattan. Every surface gets a gradient for form, a highlight and a soft contact shadow. Detail lives in the small things: tufting, piping, book spines of different heights, leaf veins.

Type

Fraunces for names and moments ("Properly homey", "The jazz den"). DM Sans for everything you tap and read. Both are already in the app.

Interface

Near-black warm ground, cream text, one amber accent for the primary action and for lit week dots. The interface stays quiet so the room can glow.

Room geometry

The existing one-point cutaway: back wall 250 × 280 px in a 390 × 600 view, side walls and floor in perspective. Every flat shares it, so items, light pools and moon patches can be placed by formula.

4The lighting model

The room is painted flat-lit, then light is added in layers. All seven layers are cheap SVG or canvas operations.

Every lamp is an item, and every item can be a light

The catalogue already tags lights with light. Brownstone gives each one a light definition. The renderer cuts one darkness hole and draws one glow per light, so placing a lamp visibly lights its corner, and the "tap a slot to place it" flow becomes the moment a corner comes alive.

"light": {
  "x": 0.5, "y": 0.1,        // emitter, relative to the item box
  "radius": 90,              // px at the back wall; scaled by depth
  "warmth": 2400,            // kelvin, 1800 (candle) to 4000 (bare bulb)
  "pattern": "dapple",       // none | cone | dapple | stars | stripes | flicker
  "throw": "down"            // down | up | wall | room
}

Patterns are the signature of a style. A pierced brass lantern scatters star-shaped spots across the ceiling. A wicker dome dapples. Shutters stripe. Candles flicker. A flat with one pattern light already looks like someone's home.

The room gets warmer as it fills

The window light stays

The cold moonlit window is the one thing that never changes, and it is the strongest part of the look. What changes is how much it dominates. Window treatments shape it instead of removing it:

  • Bare sash: the full patch with crisp mullion shadows.
  • Curtains: narrow the patch by about a third and soften its edge.
  • Louvred shutters: break it into stripes across the rug.
  • Roller blind: shortens the patch to its near half.
  • Plants on the sill: add blurred leaf shadows inside it.

That makes the moonlight something people decorate, not just a light that fades.

5Progression in four stages

The kit shows your flat at four moments. They are illustrations of a continuous system, not gates the player has to pass.

0 items · Bare little box

Day 1

Stained grey walls, scuffed boards, one bare bulb, boxes marked "BOOKS?". The moonlight from the uncurtained window is the main light. Darkness 0.66, full cool tint.

6 items · Snug nook

Unpacked

Walls still grey, moonlight still dominant. A paper globe softens the bulb, and a tripod lamp beside a secondhand mustard armchair makes the first warm pool. That cold-against-warm contrast is the emotional beat of week one.

14 items · Dreamy den

Settled

The bottle-green paint is the big moment. Velvet curtains narrow the moonlight. The globe, tripod lamp and striped rug from week one are still there, so the things you own visibly carry forward.

23 items · Storybook flat

Homey

Many small warm sources take over: arc lamp, pendant, sconce, picture light, fairy lights, candles. The moonlight is a quiet accent on the rug. Darkness 0.55, almost no tint.

Design rule

Items persist between stages and are never swapped for better versions behind the player's back. The tripod lamp you unpacked on day two is still in the corner on day sixty. Progress is addition and warming, not replacement.

6Big moments and quiet weeks

Surfaces are the big moments

Paint, curtains and flooring change the whole room in one go, which makes them feel bigger than any single lamp. Offer them as tier 3 choices, as streak milestones, or as the reward for a first four-workout week. The first paint colour is the clearest "this is mine now" moment in the game.

Quiet weeks dim, never take

After seven quiet days (when the pet already turns sleepy), lamps dim to about 70%. After three quiet weeks they sit at candle level with the pet asleep in the glow. One logged workout turns every lamp back on at once. Nothing is removed, and the room still looks lovely when dim.

Lights switch on

Placing a light plays a short warm-up: the darkness hole opens and the glow blooms over about 600 ms. Logging a workout can do the same for every lamp, which turns the daily tap into a small visible reward.

Real time, optional

Night is the default. Later, the moon patch could follow the local clock: blue hour at dusk, deep night after one in the morning, a thin dawn shaft. It is atmosphere only and never gates anything.

A short wall list

Six curated paints: green, oxblood, terracotta, navy, ochre and plum. All are mid-dark and saturated enough to glow under warm light. No white or pale grey, which would read as flat under the darkness layer.

Window position comes free

A unit's window sits where its facade window is: left, centre or right. The moon patch lands somewhere different in every flat before anyone decorates.

A hero slot

One large floor slot per flat for a statement piece. It sets the personality and suggests the room's name.

Auto-matched colours

Item palettes already have primary, shade and accent roles. Shift the primary slightly toward a harmonious hue for the current wall, derive the shade from it, and keep metals (brass, black) fixed as accents. Mixing items from different styles then still looks intended.

8The street and visiting

The building view is a laptop-wide night street: one brownstone with six windows, a stoop with iron railings, a brass plaque showing the invite code, street lamps, wet reflections and a neon "Flats" sign on the roof.

  • Window glow scales with that friend's workouts this week, from a faint cold pane at zero to blazing amber with interior silhouettes at seven.
  • Window boxes grow one flower per workout this week, with the resident's name stencilled on the box.
  • Stars left by visitors are nailed to the window frame as small brass stars and stay there.
  • Empty units show a "Room for a friend" card in the window with the invite action.
  • Time, weather and season change the street only: dusk, late night, rain, snow, autumn leaves.

Visiting opens the neighbour's flat at full detail with a small panel at the bottom: Heart, Star, and "Treat for" the pet's name. Each can be sent once a day, and the panel says what each one does ("Stars stay on Maya's window frame from the street").

10Implementation notes

  • Projection. All flats use one formula, so lights, patches and slots can be computed: a point at room coordinates (X, Y, Z) maps to [195 + X·s, 280 + (1.92 − Y)·s] with s = 312.5 / (5 − Z).
  • Data. Add an optional light object to light-tagged items, a moonShape modifier to window items, and a windowSide to each unit. Paint becomes a surface kind beside wallpaper and flooring.
  • Rendering. The current room is SVG, so the stack can stay SVG: one mask with a radial hole per light, one screen-blend group, one moon polygon. Cap visible light holes at about twelve and merge the faintest ones on low-end phones.
  • Grain and blend modes. The grain filter is the heaviest layer. Render it once to a cached image or a CSS background rather than as a live SVG filter on every frame.
  • Accessibility. The room is decorative and already described in text (title, item count, cosiness). Keep that, and keep all controls outside the dark room so their contrast never depends on lighting.
  • Art pipeline. Items keep the catalogue's layered-primitive format. Brownstone needs more layers per item (highlights, piping, grain), so a small number of hand-drawn SVG items may be cheaper than extending the primitive renderer.

11Open questions

  1. Should paint replace the current wallpaper surfaces, or sit alongside them as a separate, rarer reward?
  2. How many lights before the room feels overlit? The Homey showcase uses about ten sources.
  3. Do we follow the real clock for the moon, or keep it always night?
  4. Is the hero slot one fixed location for every flat, or does it move with the window side?
  5. How do bunny, frog and duck fit the adult tone? The kit draws them naturalistically, not as cartoons.
  6. Does dimming after quiet weeks ever feel like punishment in testing? If it does, drop it and keep only the sleepy pet.
